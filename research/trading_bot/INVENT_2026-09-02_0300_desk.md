## Angle 1 — ETF share-class premium fade — DEAD AT SCREEN (family closure)

**Mechanism.** 2025–26 wave of ETF share classes of existing mutual funds (post-Vanguard
patent). The ETF class has an *exact* NAV anchor (the mutual fund's own 4pm NAV, free
daily). New tiny ETF classes could close at premium/discount; fade to the anchor.

**Kill (generic, pre-data).** Wherever creations/redemptions are OPEN, AP arbitrage
bounds any close dislocation at roughly the AP's round-trip cost — single-digit bp on
liquid large-cap underlyings — while OUR toll is the retail quoted spread on a sub-$50M
new ETF (tens of bp). The exploitable residual is bounded by (AP cost) − (our toll) < 0
**by construction**, before any sample is drawn. Any measured "premium" beyond that is
the in-sample-fill mirage (SWF wall): it is quoted spread, not capturable edge.

**Family closure recorded:** *every* ETF premium/discount close-fade variant with open
creations is dead by the AP-bound argument — this closes the 08-31 wrapper-fade instance
generically. The only instruments where price can detach beyond AP cost are those with
creations halted/restricted: CEFs (dead: NAV-innovation, non-US = owner spend), ETNs
(issuer-credit + published), and **wind-down ETFs — which is Angle 2**.

## Angle 2 — ETF liquidation terminal convergence — ADMISSIBLE → PROPOSAL (no build yet)

**Mechanism (structural, date-certain, long-only).** When an ETF announces closure
(30–60 day notice via 497 supplement + press release, both free on EDGAR), creations
stop or become restricted near the end → the AP bound of Angle 1 *lifts* → the price can
trade at a real discount to NAV as holders dump into a bid vacuum. The terminal value is
a date-certain CASH distribution ≈ final NAV (paid 3–5 business days post-delisting).
Buy the discount after the announcement, hold to cash. Long-only (no borrow wall), not
latency, not directional prediction — the same synchronous-mark/terminal-value shape as
the programme's only real wins. ~150–200 US closures/yr.

**Published-edge screen (done this tick, before pinning).** Two bounded searches found
no published "buy the wind-down discount" strategy: practitioner coverage (Schwab,
ETF.com, Kiplinger, justETF) treats holding-to-liquidation as a passive *option* and
mostly advises selling early; academic work covers closure *determinants* (ScienceDirect
2018; 1995–2018 survival study), not discount capture. PASS — self-invented.

**The honest trap, stated up front.** The discount may be FAIR compensation: wind-down
transaction costs are borne partly by holders (documented extreme: SPA UK ETFs cost
holders ~10 % of NAV), and last-seen NAV can overstate the final cash. Therefore the
test target is **realized final cash per share vs entry price** — never the quoted
discount. Capacity honesty: median liquidated ETF ≈ $5.7M AUM; even a PASS is a
small-dollar arm (~$1–5k/event without moving it), not a season-maker.

**Why forward collection.** Retrospective free data fails: delisted tickers drop off
free price APIs, historical daily NAV for dead funds is not freely archived, and final
per-share cash amounts live in scattered press releases. The programme's standard
escape applies (T31/T33 playbook): a $0 forward paper logger.

**Pre-registered forward design (pinned NOW, before any number):**
- Universe: every US-listed ETF/ETN closure announced after the collector starts.
  Event source: EDGAR full-text 497/497K liquidation supplements + issuer press pages.
- Log per event: announcement date, last trading day, daily 4pm price + issuer NAV from
  announcement to delisting, quoted spread at each log, final cash per share and pay
  date from the issuer's liquidation press release.
- Simulated entry: buy at the **ask** on announcement+1 close (full half-spread toll,
  causal); exit: final cash distribution (no exit toll). No intermediate marking counts.
- PASS: N ≥ 30 completed events, pooled mean net return > 0 with cluster-bootstrap
  (cluster = issuer × month) CI-lo > 0. Charge as trial 1 of its own family.
- K1 (feasibility kill): < 4 events with complete free data in the first 21 collecting
  days → BLOCKED-ON-DATA, log and stop.
- K2 (economics kill): at N ≥ 30, CI-lo ≤ 0 → DEAD; also DEAD early if pooled mean net
  < −1 % at N ≥ 10 (fair-compensation confirmed).
- K3 (tail kill): any single event worse than −15 % net → flag; 2 such → DEAD (SPA-class
  wind-down cost risk dominates).
