## A1 — Rule 201 restriction-state reconstruction from `short_exempt_volume` — **DEAD AT SCREEN**

**Mechanism.** SEC Rule 201 imposes a *dated, mechanical, non-predictive* constraint: when a stock
falls ≥10% from the prior close, short sales may only be executed **above the national best bid**
for the remainder of that session **and the whole of the next session**. For that window the most
aggressive short supply is removed by rule; at the next open it is restored. That is a structural
forced-flow event of exactly the kind this programme's only wins have come from — except there is
**no free public list of which symbols are restricted on which day**. The invention was that the
ignored `short_exempt_volume` column *reconstructs* it for free: exempt marking is what a bona-fide
market maker's hedging short sale gets while the restriction is in force, so the exempt share of
volume should spike on restricted days. This would have **manufactured an otherwise-unobservable
free dataset out of a column nobody trades**.

**The screen (X-side only — no return was touched).** The mechanism makes a hard, falsifiable
prediction about the *shape* of the flagged set that needs no prices at all: because the restriction
covers the trigger day plus the whole next session, genuine restriction episodes must appear as runs
of **exactly 2 consecutive flagged sessions**, and the rarer/cleaner the threshold, the more the runs
should concentrate on 2. Measured over the last 120 sessions, 682,760 symbol-days with total volume
≥ 50k:

| exempt/total threshold | flagged symbol-days | per session | runs of len 1 | len 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| >1%   | 81,782 (11.98%) | 681.5 | **67%** | 20% |
| >10%  | 9,695 | 80.8 | **81%** | 13% |
| >25%  | 1,005 | 8.4  | **93%** | 5% |
| >50%  | 128   | 1.1  | **98%** | 2% |

**Verdict: the identifying assumption is false, and it fails in the direction that cannot be
rescued.** The flagged population is overwhelmingly *one-day* episodes, and it becomes **more**
one-day as the threshold is tightened — the exact opposite of the mechanism's prediction. At >25%
the per-session count (8.4) is finally the right order of magnitude for real Rule 201 triggers, but
93% of those episodes last a single session, so whatever that column is marking, it is not the
two-day regulatory state. The exempt field is dominated by idiosyncratic one-day exempt marking
(riskless principal, VWAP/derivative-hedge exemptions, odd-lot handling), not by the circuit breaker.
**Killed on the X side alone, before a single return was examined** — the cheapest possible death.

## A2 — FINRA short-interest `revisionFlag` as a correction event — **DEAD AT SCREEN (mechanism)**

**Mechanism.** FINRA occasionally revises an already-published short-interest figure. If anyone
positions on short interest, they held a wrong number for a fortnight and the revision is new
information published on a dated schedule.

**The screen.** Base rate across all 207 settlement files, 3,904,106 rows: **28,158 rows carry
`revisionFlag`** (0.72%), ≈136 revisions per settlement date. So the event is **not** power-blocked —
N is ample, which is why it was worth ten seconds to check rather than assume.

**Verdict: killed on mechanism, not on N.** The trade requires the revision to *change someone's
position*, which requires the original number to have been load-bearing at a two-week horizon. Short
interest is a slow, weakly-priced, heavily-published quantity; a 0.7%-frequency clerical correction
to a slow signal is not news, and pricing it would be a directional bet on a micro-revision — the
same family as the **0-for-8 directional record**. Filed dead so the next round does not re-find it.
Recorded honestly: this is a *judgment* kill, not a measured one. It can be reopened for the cost of
one run if a future tick wants the number rather than the argument.

## A3 — **SETTLE-THROUGH (STICKYSHORT)**: separating committed short positioning from market-maker churn — **ADMISSIBLE → PRE-REGISTERED, TEST DEFERRED**

**Mechanism, and what is actually new.** FINRA daily short volume is ~45–50% of all volume, and it
is retail folklore ("short volume ratio") precisely because people read it as bearish positioning.
It is mostly not: the overwhelming majority is intraday market-making that is flat by the close and
**never settles into a short position**. The two free panels can be differenced to say how much of a
fortnight's short flow actually *stuck*:

    STR_i = ( SI_i(t) - SI_i(t-1) ) / ( sum of daily short volume over the sessions in (t-1, t] )

High STR = shorting that was **carried overnight and settled** — committed, capital-consuming,
directional. Low STR = the same headline short volume that was pure inventory churn carrying no
information. The invention is the **normalizer**: the published short-interest anomaly scales short
position by shares outstanding or float; the folklore indicator uses the raw short-volume ratio.
Dividing the *change in settled position* by the *flow that produced it* is a different object from
both, and it is the one that isolates conviction from noise.

**Is the object real? (X-side screen, no returns.)** Last complete window (settlement 2026-07-15 →
2026-07-31, 12 sessions, 21,793 symbols in both files; filters: cumulative short volume > 100k
shares, ΔSI > 0) → **N = 3,367** usable names with

    p10 = 0.011   p25 = 0.034   p50 = 0.090   p75 = 0.190   p90 = 0.342   p99 = 0.936

The median name settles only **9%** of its fortnightly short flow, and the cross-section spans **30×**
from p10 to p90. The decomposition is therefore neither degenerate nor a rescaling of the headline
ratio — there is a real, wide, free cross-sectional signal here. **This is the only thing measured
this tick; no return was conditioned on it.**

**Pre-registration (BINDING; frozen before any return is looked at).**

- *Data.* `research/data/newterr_shortint/panels.npz` — 3,812 symbols × 2,669 sessions (2016-01 →
  2026-08), `si_pos/si_prev` at 207 settlement dates, daily `sv_short`, `is_etf`, and critically
  `si_avail_idx` (the session index at which each SI figure became **public**). All entries key off
  `si_avail_idx`, never off `si_settle` — the publication lag is the leakage guard.
- *Universe at each t.* `is_etf == False`; cumulative short volume in the window > 100,000 shares;
  close ≥ $5 at `si_avail_idx[t]`; median dollar volume over the window ≥ $1M.
- *Signal.* STR as defined above, restricted to ΔSI > 0. Quintiles, equal-weight.
- *Sign, fixed now.* **Long Q1** (lowest STR — flow was churn, no information) / **short Q5**
  (highest STR — committed settled shorting is informed). A sign flip found later is a kill, not a
  finding.
- *Timing.* Enter at the **open of the session after `si_avail_idx[t]`**; hold to the open of the
  session after `si_avail_idx[t+1]`. ~26 observations/year. No intraday, no lookahead.
- *Costs.* Charge **10 bp per side per name actually traded**, applied to **measured** turnover — do
  not assume 100%. At 26 rebalances/yr this is a drag of order 5%/yr and it is expected to be the
  binding constraint.
- *Split.* Design half = settlement dates 1–103 (2017-12 → ~2022-03). **Held-out half = 104–207,
  one shot, zero tuning after it is touched.**
- *PASS requires all three.* (i) held-out net-of-cost mean > 0 with a **cluster bootstrap by
  settlement date**, CI-lo > 0; (ii) **net annualized ≥ 3%** — the distance-from-zero bar, because a
  surviving 0.5%/yr is dead at any capacity; (iii) **subsumption control** — regress the STR
  long-short return on the plain SI/float quintile long-short return and require the **alpha's**
  CI-lo > 0. Failing (iii) means we rediscovered the published short-interest anomaly under a new
  normalizer, i.e. a **crowded** edge, and it is killed.
- *Kills.* **K1** usable N < 300 names in a period → dead on breadth. **K2** held-out CI-lo ≤ 0.
  **K3** net annualized < 3% even with CI-lo > 0 → dead at the friction floor. **K4** fails
  subsumption → dead as crowded. **K5** sign flips between design and held-out half.
- *Multiple-testing cost.* This round declares 3 angles; two died on the X side/mechanism **without a
  return test**, so the return-test count increments by **+1**, not +3. The bar is the pre-committed
  single held-out shot plus the size floor, not a p-value hunt.

**Power, stated honestly and up front.** 207 settlement dates → ~103 held-out portfolio observations.
The smallest per-observation Sharpe distinguishable from zero at one-sided 5% is
1.645/√103 ≈ 0.162, i.e. **≈0.83 annualized**. This test **can only confirm a large edge**, and a
null result is *not* evidence of absence — it is evidence that the edge, if any, is smaller than a
bi-monthly panel can see. That limit is the wall FINDINGS already named for this family (§L560); it
is a reason to state the size bar in advance, which is what (ii) does.

**Survivorship, stated as unresolved.** The panel's 3,812 symbols are a *current* universe, so
delisted names are absent. The sign of the resulting bias is genuinely unclear — dropping
shorted-to-zero delistings removes the short leg's best outcomes (conservative), while dropping
acquired-at-a-premium names removes its worst (anti-conservative). We do not claim to know which
dominates. This is a second, independent reason the PASS bar requires the effect to be **large**.

**Honest prior, written before the run.** The published short-interest anomaly is of order 5–8%/yr
gross on decile long-shorts; ours is a coarser quintile sort carrying ~5%/yr of measured cost.
**The expected outcome is death at the friction floor, like the previous eight.** The case for
running it anyway is that it is $0 on a panel that already exists, the normalizer is genuinely
uncrowded, and the death is worth logging either way — this family has been sitting untested in
FINDINGS since T16 built it.

**Freeze-then-test discipline: the test is NOT run this tick.** Designing and reading a result in the
same tick is exactly the failure mode this programme has rules against. Filed as agenda **T58**.
